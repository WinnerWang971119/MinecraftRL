## The best-checkpoint selector kept the weakest of the six perfect nets

`runs/m4.best.pt` is eval 3 — the FIRST net to score 1.000, at grad_step 8307, killing
in a mean 37.4 steps. Evals 4-8 also scored 1.000, with mean lengths of 20.1, 27.4,
24.4, 22.7 and 24.2 — up to **1.9x more decisive on the same score**.

The selector advances on strict improvement (`>`), and 1.000 never exceeds 1.000, so it
never revisited. There is no tie-break on decisiveness. **`m4.best.pt` is a perfect net,
but not the best perfect net**, and nothing in the run's output says so.

Anyone choosing a warm start or a demo checkpoint should weigh the periodic saves around
grad_step 15,000-21,000 against `best.pt`, not assume the filename settles it.

| Mean episode length (greedy eval) | — | 76.45, **median 17.0** |
| Damage dealt per eval episode | — | 19.7 of 20 HP |
| Damage taken per eval episode | — | **2.2 HP** |

**Throughput was ~3x the assumption.** The 6h34m run reached its 30,000-step budget in
about half the projected time.

**Mean training episode length was ~95 steps, not 285** (30,503 episodes against the
measured 122 transitions/s). `eps_decay_episodes` is derived from that 285. Anything
sized off it is sized for a regime that does not exist.

**The mean/median split is the real story of the eval:** mean 76.45, median **17.0**.
The typical fight is over in 17 decision steps — 3.4 seconds. The mean is dragged up
entirely by the handful of episodes that hit the 400-step cap. Reporting the mean alone
makes the agent look four times slower than it is.

## Two caveats that make the numbers softer than they look

**1. The opponent had no weapon.** `spawn_dummy_pad.mcfunction` never armed it; the
bridge's `dummyResetTemplate` was `inventory: []`. It punched for 1 damage against the
learner's 6-damage iron sword — which is why damage taken averaged 2.2 HP per episode,
about two connected punches across a whole fight. A 1.000 win rate here is not evidence
of skill against an armed opponent. Fixed on `feat/m4-selfplay` (T2/T3).

**2. Three observation features were dead.** `opp_vel_local` (indices 14-16) is read
through the learner's mineflayer client, which never updates a walking entity's velocity
on 1.21.1 — `entity_velocity` fires only on knockback, and `rel_entity_move` translates
position only. So this agent could not perceive whether its opponent was moving, and
neither could any agent in `runs/`. Being fixed as T20.

- `aim_while_invisible = 0.000` throughout — no spin-farming, on every cycle

## Verdict

The stack works and the agent is strong against the scripted opponent — undefeated over
200 evaluation episodes, clearing the M2 bar outright six times. But it was strong
against a target that could barely hurt it, while blind to opponent motion. Both are
fixed on the self-play branch, which is precisely why this run's win rates should not be
read as a baseline for the armored self-play run: **it is a different game.**
